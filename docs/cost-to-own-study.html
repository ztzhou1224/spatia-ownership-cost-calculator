Cost to own a home: where we can estimate it, and the product we should build

The pilot at cost.runspatia.com estimates every cost line in all 50 states and DC. The property tax decides how good the total is, and its quality differs by state. This page measures it per state and per line, then gives the full design for a stand-alone product.

Measured from the calculator's data build (data service build 20261118T210647Z, Census ZCTA files, Freddie Mac PMMS 2026-11-12) and spatia-data's jurisdiction record.

The answer

We can give a full estimate in 51 of 51 places today. Three of them are strong on tax; in 48 the tax is an area average. With the steps below, 48 can move up one grade.

1A · TestedFlorida. The tax has a back-tested range in 27 of 67 counties, plus the parcel's flat charges and tax district from an address.
2B · Rule and published ratesCalifornia (1 % of the price by law, plus voter rates) and Texas (each taxing unit's published rate). No tested range.
39C · Area rateA sale does not change the taxed value. The area's average rate is a fair start; the current owner's bill is a better one.
9D · Area rate, likely lowA sale ends a cap, so a buyer pays more than today's owners: AR, DC, GA, MD, MI, NM, OK, SC, WY.

The other lines (loan, insurance, energy, upkeep) are national and do not change the grade. Their gaps are listed in the next section.

How to raise the grade

39 states · C

Use the current owner's bill

Where a sale does not change the taxed value, the seller's latest bill is the best start. RentCast's record gives it for most homes. The bill includes the seller's exemptions, so it can be a little low; the page says so.

Cost: no new data. Risk: low. We have not measured how often RentCast has a bill.

9 places · D

Use the state's sales-ratio study

Where a sale ends a cap, the buyer's value is the assessor's market value. Most states publish a yearly ratio of assessed value to sale price per county. That ratio times the price, times local rates, gives the buyer's tax.

Cost: one spatia-data layer per state, plus rate tables. Risk: medium.

DC, OH

Port Home's tested estimates

Spatia Home already has back-tested estimates for the District (1,486 sales) and two Ohio counties (Franklin, Cuyahoga). The calculator can reuse them.

Cost: small. Risk: low.

CA · TX · FL

Finish the three strong states

  • California: read more county rate books (2 of 58 today).
  • Texas: special-district boundaries (MUDs) so they are found, not ticked.
  • Florida: back-test the other 40 counties; typical flat charges for a ZIP-only search.

A back-test needs public sale prices. Eleven states do not publish them: AK, ID, KS, LA, MO, MS, MT, NM, TX, UT, WY. There, a tax figure can follow the law and the rates, but it cannot show a tested range.

Part two

The product: a stand-alone design

Job

One question

"What will this home really cost me each month, all in?" Answered for one address in under five seconds, with every line sourced and every input changeable.

Who

Readers

  • Buyers before an offer (main audience).
  • Buyer's agents and loan officers who send a link to a client.
  • People moving to another state who compare places.
Why us

What free sites do not show

  • The tax a new owner pays, not today's average rate.
  • The flood zone, flat charges and home facts from the records at the address.
  • A tested range where one exists, and the source of every figure.

The main flow

  1. Type an address (or a ZIP for the area only).
  2. See the monthly total, the range and the share of each line.
  3. Open a line to see its source, its range and what changes it.
  4. Change an input or type your own figure; the total updates at once.
  5. Save or share the result, or compare it with a second home.
  6. Get the full Home report for this address (the paid next step).

The result screen

One screen, answer first. The total and the breakdown sit at the top; each line opens in place; the inputs sit in a side panel on a computer and in a sheet on a phone.

Product rules

Honest figures

  • A range only where a back-test measured it.
  • Each figure names its source and year.
  • No estimate is called a quote.
  • The tax says which grade it is, in words.

Privacy

  • The address is not stored or logged.
  • No owner name, agent or MLS field is read.
  • Analytics see the page, never the address.

Cost control

  • Each address costs two RentCast calls (about $0.06 each, from Home's measurement): about $0.12 a lookup.
  • Today: 10 lookups a minute per visitor; no daily cap.
  • Needed: a daily cap and a short cache by address hash.

One Worker serves the pages and one lookup API. The data tables are rebuilt from the data service by a script. Nothing about the reader is stored. To add saved results and a daily cap, the Worker needs one KV namespace.

Decisions for you

D1 · Name and address

The pilot runs at cost.runspatia.com. A product name helps the search pages and the links people share.

RecommendedA. "Spatia Cost to Own" at cost.runspatia.comNo change of address; the name says the job. Low cost.
B. A new domainEasier to remember, but a new domain starts with no search history and costs a yearly fee.

After A: I change the title, the header and the metadata. After B: you buy the domain; I move the Worker to it.

D2 · Who may look up an address

Owner, 19 November 2026: the site is free with no login; it records nothing the visitor enters and shows no privacy notice. The calculation runs in the browser. What stays open is the cost: each address lookup costs about $0.12, and a free, open lookup can cost a lot if a bot finds it.

RecommendedA. Free, with a daily capFree for everyone up to a cap (for example $20 a day, about 160 lookups). Above it, the ZIP estimate still works. Needs one KV namespace.
C. No capThe most use, and no limit on the RentCast bill.

After A: I create the KV namespace and the cap, and show the cap's use on the operator side.

D3 · The tax default in the 39 C states

Today these states use the area's average rate. The current owner's bill (from RentCast) is closer for most homes, but it carries the seller's exemptions.

RecommendedA. The current bill when RentCast has itThe default becomes the latest bill, labelled "the current owner's bill"; the area rate stays as the fallback.
B. Keep the area rateOne method everywhere; the bill stays a note.

After A: I measure on 50 addresses how often a bill exists and how it compares with the area rate, then switch.

D4 · The paid next step

The calculator brings people who are about to buy. The Home report is the paid product.

RecommendedA. One link to the Home report"Full report for this home, $7.99" on every address result. No partner leads.
B. Add partner offersInsurance or loan quotes. More income, but partners and their terms are a new business.

After A: I add the link with the address passed to Home's preview.

D5 · State and county guides

Search pages bring visitors at no cost per visit, as Spatia Crime does.

RecommendedA. Build them in Phase 4About 3,200 pages from the existing tables, each with its sources.
B. Not nowKeep the site to the calculator.
